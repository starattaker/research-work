\begin{abstract}
We study end-to-end alveolar bone-loss \emph{severity} measurement on intra-oral periapical (IOPA) radiographs from the DenPAR dataset (1{,}000 images, 4{,}402 teeth). Our contributions are threefold: (1) a \textbf{region-growing} multipass assignment of expert CEJ/apex clicks to tooth masks (1\,px rings up to 8--12\,px), improving label retention versus fixed-margin heuristics; (2) \textbf{PCA-axis slot labeling} for mesial/distal keypoint pairing; and (3) \textbf{axis-constrained severity}, projecting CEJ, bone intersection, and apex onto a tooth long axis before applying the bone-loss ratio (alternative to the paper's min-max line through three points). A YOLOv8x detector and three Keypoint R-CNN heads (CEJ, intersection, apex) achieve test OKS of 0.927 / 0.894 / 0.871 (paper: 0.954 / 0.912 / 0.815). End-to-end severity ICC on the test split reaches \textbf{$\sim$0.73} versus the published target of 0.801 under honest slot-index pairing. \placeholder{Insert axis-method ICC table after GPU run}. An extended clinical cohort of 214 IOPA images annotated by collaborating clinicians is reserved for future external validation pending data-use approval.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Alveolar bone loss severity on radiographs is a core periodontal endpoint. DenPAR \cite{denpar2026} provides a public benchmark with tooth boxes, CEJ/apex clicks, bone-level lines, and segmentation masks, and reports high agreement between an automated pipeline and experts (ICC $\approx$ 0.80 on severity). Replicating this benchmark requires careful treatment of \emph{label construction}---expert clicks often fall just outside mask boundaries---and of \emph{severity geometry}---how three anatomical points define a percentage bone loss along the root.

This work documents our replication and extensions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Systematic preprocessing ablations (v1--v7) quantifying click retention and downstream keypoint OKS.
  \item A production pipeline (v6) combining region-growing assignment, PCA-axis slots, and endpoint-based intersection labels.
  \item Axis-constrained severity variants (mask PCA; CEJ--intersection midpoint axis) evaluated against the paper's Eq.~1 formulation.
  \item End-to-end ICC evaluation with val-locked hyperparameter search over pairing protocols and apex-merge radii.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Scope.} We address detection, keypoint estimation, and severity ICC. Segmentation beyond tooth masks, pattern classification, and full clinical deployment are out of scope.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

DenPAR \cite{denpar2026} uses YOLO-style detection and Keypoint R-CNN for CEJ, intersection, and apex, then derives severity from geometric ratios. Instance segmentation approaches (Mask R-CNN \cite{he2017mask}) motivate our mask-centric label assignment. We use Ultralytics YOLOv8 \cite{jocher2023yolo} for detection and torchvision Keypoint R-CNN for anatomical points.

\subsubsection{v6 slot labeling}
CEJ and apex clicks sorted into two slots by projection onto the mask PCA long axis (not x-sort padding). Intersection: bone-line endpoints assigned to nearest tooth mask, max two per tooth (left/right bone lines).

\subsubsection{v7 ablation}
Point-assignment sweep recommended \texttt{max\_grace\_px=12}, \texttt{bbox\_outlier\_margin\_px=7.6}. Retraining on v7 labels did not improve intersection OKS (0.882 vs.\ 0.894 on v6).

\subsection{Severity measurement}
\label{sec:severity}

\subsubsection{Paper Eq.~1 (baseline)}
Given CEJ $\mathbf{c}$, intersection $\mathbf{i}$, apex $\mathbf{a}$: sort by $x$, fit min-max line through three points, project, and compute
\begin{equation}
\text{severity\%} = 100 \cdot \frac{\|\mathrm{proj}(\mathbf{c})-\mathrm{proj}(\mathbf{i})\|}{\|\mathrm{proj}(\mathbf{c})-\mathrm{proj}(\mathbf{a})\|}.
\end{equation}
Invalid geometry (intersection not between CEJ and apex) is rejected; output clipped to $[0,100]$.

\subsubsection{Axis-constrained severity (proposed)}
\textbf{(A) Mask PCA axis:} centroid and major PCA direction of the tooth mask; project points onto the axis; same ratio along scalar coordinates.

\textbf{(B) CEJ--INT midpoint axis:} origin at midpoint of visible CEJ pair; direction from CEJ midpoint toward intersection midpoint.

Slot index 0/1 pairs mesial/distal sides (v6 PCA labels). ICC uses \texttt{match\_by\_slot} pairing.

  \item Combine tensor slots; pair GT/pred by slot index.
  \item Val-locked grid: combine $\times$ protocol $\times$ apex\_merge $\in \{8,\ldots,32\}$\,px.
  \item Report train/val/test ICC(2,1).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{ICC gap ($\sim$0.07).} Oracle ICC $\approx$0.79 indicates keypoints are largely informative; remaining error is YOLO box shift, intersection localization, and protocol sensitivity---not wholly model incapacity.

\textbf{v7 negative result.} More assigned training clicks (12\,px grace) did not improve intersection OKS; severe overfitting after epoch $\sim$5 across all v7 heads.

\textbf{Axis methods.} Axis-constrained severity is clinically motivated: bone loss is measured along the root, not an arbitrary min-max line. \placeholder{Report whether PCA or CEJ--INT midpoint ICC exceeds Eq.~1}.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a reproducible DenPAR severity pipeline with region-growing label assignment, PCA-axis slots, and axis-constrained severity variants. Keypoint OKS approaches paper benchmarks; honest end-to-end ICC reaches $\sim$0.73. Future work: external validation on the 214-image cohort, intersection head refinement, and axis-method ICC comparison.
